Thesis
The son or daughter who worries is the one who pays: they give Mum a card at Christmas, she writes a few wishes at her own pace, and all they're ever told is "Mum has started".
Who it's for
Buyer: an adult child, 35–55, who has been meaning to "have the conversation". User: their parent, 65–85, comfortable with a phone but not an app person.
What's cut
Diagnosis path, Grief Support, Memorial, the will interview, find help nearby, people to call, songs and own song, Reflection and "what you want to achieve", the legal guide, the dashboard, pricing tiers. About three-quarters of today's demo.
What's kept
A short gift checkout; one card with one code; Mari's Farewell & Ceremony and Personal Touches questions, one per screen; the "not a will" line; one switch the parent controls to say "I've started". Next in line: where things are kept (Mari's eight document types, notes only).
MVP for the Christmas vouchers
100 printed cards, each with its own word-and-number code, handed to friends and family to give. A redeem page, three wishes, and a "Mum has started" email sent by hand from a list. No checkout yet: the buyer screen is the brief for the paid version.
How it makes money
One-off gift paid by the child via Stripe (draft £39, posted card included). Parent's wishes stay free for good. Second gifts (Dad, an aunt) and Mother's Day / Father's Day are the repeat sale.
The one metric
Started rate: share of cards redeemed with at least three wishes written within 30 days of Christmas.
The biggest risk
A gift about death lands badly, so the card goes in a drawer. Nobody sells this as a gift yet: it is untested, which is both the gap and the danger.
