Thesis
A life event makes couples ask "what if one of us…?" — so plan it as two people, side by side, and turn the gaps into one good conversation.
Who it's for
Couples and households (often unmarried, often new parents or new homeowners) planning jointly, aged roughly 28–45.
What's cut
Memorial, Grief Support, songs, soundtrack, own song, reflection & values, bucket list, diagnosis path and advance care, funeral options explainer, find help nearby, people to call, will interview, memories, the gift offer. About two thirds of today's demo.
What's kept
Mari's life-situation guidance, a short set of her farewell questions, the will / executors / pension-nomination guidance, Important Documents (now shared), plus new: the partner invite and the side-by-side reveal.
MVP for the Christmas vouchers
One voucher = one household. Situation quiz → invite partner by text link → 8 questions each, answered privately → the reveal → "talk about this" cards → shared "where things are kept" → a printable plan for two. No vault uploads, no release.
How it makes money
A one-off household plan for two (draft: £49), free with a voucher. Longer term, paid by partners who meet couples at the moment: mortgage brokers, antenatal classes, life insurers, will-writers (referral fee).
The one metric
Second-partner completion: the share of started households where the invited partner finishes their answers and the reveal is opened.
The biggest risk
The invited partner never joins, leaving a half-plan; and couples at a happy life event may not want to talk about death at all.

Not a legal will. A human one.

Living together, unmarried: there is no such thing as common-law marriage. An unmarried partner has no automatic right to inherit — however many years you have shared. A will is essential to protect them.

Next: two wills, one each, naming each other and Isla's guardians. A solicitor is best for property and trusts; an online will suits straightforward estates; several charities fund free simple wills.
