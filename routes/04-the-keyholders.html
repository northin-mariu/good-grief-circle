Thesis
Most people don't need another planner; they need one dependable answer to "who can open this, and when?" — three keys, two of which open it, after a wait you can stop.
Who it's for
Someone 55+ who has some wishes written down (or will write a few) and wants them reaching the right people at the right time — plus the three people they choose, who are the next customers.
What's cut
Reflection, memorial, grief support space, songs and own song, will interview, the directory, the legal guide, the three-step Planning journey and the dashboard. Wishes shrink to one short card; documents shrink to "where things are kept" (no uploads).
What's kept
Choosing the circle (now exactly three keyholders), invitations, a short wishes card, a "where things are kept" list, a note to the keyholders, and Mari's first steps after a death on the released page.
MVP for the Christmas vouchers
The voucher switches on the release service free. Owner picks three keyholders, invites by text/WhatsApp/email, keyholders accept in one tap. Release is run by hand: two confirm, a person at Good Grief Circle checks the registration, owner is messaged every day of the wait.
How it makes money
A one-off release fee (draft: £39, test against £29), paid by the owner when they switch the release on — or by one of their children as a gift. No subscription; nothing ever locks once paid.
The one metric
Rings completed: the share of owners whose three keyholders have all accepted within 14 days.
The biggest risk
It's a promise that's tested years later: people may not pay for something that does nothing visible today, and we must still exist (and be trusted) when it fires.
